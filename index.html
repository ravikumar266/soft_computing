📖 What is the Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) Algorithm?

The Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) algorithm, created by Dervis Karaboga (2005), is a nature-inspired metaheuristic that models the collective intelligence of foraging honey bees. It searches for the global minimum of an objective function f(x) in a multi-dimensional search space.

🐝 1. Employed Bees

Each employed bee is assigned to a specific food source xi. It explores nearby solutions by perturbing one coordinate with a neighbor partner xk.

vi,j = xi,j + φ × (xi,j − xk,j), φ ∈ [−1, 1]

Greedy selection: moves to candidate if f(v) < f(x); otherwise increments trial counter.

👀 2. Onlooker Bees

Wait in the hive dance area and select food sources probabilistically proportional to nectar fitness via roulette wheel selection.

Pi = fiti / Σ fit, fiti = 1 / (1 + fi)

Fitter sources attract more onlookers, concentrating swarm exploitation on the most promising areas.

🧭 3. Scout Bees

Prevent trapping in local optima. If a food source fails to improve after limit trials (trials > limit), it is abandoned.

xi,j = −B + rand() × (2B), trials = 0

The bee becomes a scout and restarts with a new uniformly random position inside the bounds.
